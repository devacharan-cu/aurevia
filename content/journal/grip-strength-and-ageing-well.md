When people ask what they should measure to know how well they are ageing, they usually expect a blood test. The measures I find most telling are simpler: how strong your grip is, how quickly you can rise from a chair, how you manage a flight of stairs, and whether you can stand on one leg.

## Why these matter

Muscle mass and strength decline gradually from middle age, and faster without regular resistance exercise. Large population studies have consistently linked lower grip strength and slower walking pace with poorer health later in life. These are associations rather than predictions for any one person, but they point at something important: strength and balance are what keep people independent, and both respond well to training at almost any age.

## Four simple measures

- **Grip strength**, measured with a handheld dynamometer in clinic. We compare it with typical values for your age and sex and, more usefully, with your own result next year.
- **Chair rise**: how many times you can stand up from a chair and sit down again, arms crossed, in thirty seconds.
- **Balance**: how long you can stand on one leg, eyes open. Try it while brushing your teeth; it is a good habit as well as a test.
- **Walking pace and stairs**: whether a brisk walk or two flights leave you comfortably talking or needing to stop.

## What to do about them

The answer is almost always the same, and it is less demanding than people fear.

- **Resistance training on two or more days a week**, working the major muscle groups. Weights, bands, bodyweight exercises and classes all count. Gradually increasing the load matters more than the method.
- **Activity that makes you breathe harder**, adding up to about 150 minutes a week of moderate effort, in line with UK guidelines.
- **Balance work**, particularly over 65, or earlier after a fall or if you feel unsteady.
- **Enough protein**, spread through the day, especially as appetite falls with age.

## Starting late is still starting

People regularly begin strength training in their sixties and seventies and make meaningful gains within months. If you have a heart condition or joint problems, or have recently been unwell, it is sensible to start with advice. That is exactly what the studio on our lower ground floor is for.
