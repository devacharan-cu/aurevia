The average age of menopause in the UK is 51. Perimenopause, the transition leading up to it, commonly begins several years earlier, often in the early to mid-forties and sometimes before. Because periods can remain fairly regular in its early stages, it is frequently missed.

## The symptoms that don't sound like menopause

Hot flushes are the symptom everyone knows. In clinic, the ones women describe first are often different:

- Sleep that has become lighter and more broken, sometimes with night sweats that aren't recognised as such
- Low mood, irritability or anxiety that feels out of character
- Difficulty concentrating or finding words
- Joint aches, particularly in the morning
- Periods that are heavier, closer together or less predictable
- Reduced libido and vaginal dryness, which are common, very treatable, and often left unmentioned

Many of these have other possible causes, which is why a careful history matters. Thyroid problems, iron deficiency and depression can overlap with perimenopause and are worth considering.

## Do you need a blood test?

For women over 45 with typical symptoms, usually not. Hormone levels fluctuate widely during perimenopause, so a single normal result does not rule it out. The diagnosis is made from the history. Blood tests are more useful under 45, or where the picture is less clear.

## Treatment is a conversation, not a default

Hormone replacement therapy is the most effective treatment for many menopausal symptoms, and for most women under 60 the benefits are considered to outweigh the risks. That general statement is only the start. The right choice depends on your own history: migraine, clotting risk, breast cancer in the family, and what you actually want to treat.

There are also non-hormonal options, and approaches to sleep, exercise and mood that help alongside HRT or instead of it. Vaginal oestrogen, used locally for dryness and urinary symptoms, is very low risk and suitable for most women, including many who cannot take systemic HRT.
