Some of my patients spend more nights a year in hotels than at home. For them, travel health isn't a holiday checklist; it is part of ordinary life. These are the notes I find myself giving most often.

## Jet lag

Jet lag happens because your internal clock is still running on the time zone you left. It adjusts, but slowly, and usually more easily flying west than east.

- **Light is the strongest lever.** After an eastbound flight, morning light at your destination helps shift your clock earlier. After a westbound flight, seek light in the late afternoon and evening.
- **For trips of two or three days**, it can be better not to adjust at all: stay close to home time and schedule important meetings accordingly.
- **Caffeine** helps alertness, not the clock itself. Use it early in the local day.
- **Melatonin** helps some travellers, particularly eastbound. In the UK it is prescription-only, so ask us about timing and dose rather than guessing.

## Hydration and alcohol

Cabin air is dry, and it is easy to drink very little on a long flight. Mild dehydration contributes to headaches and tiredness. Water through the flight is sensible. Alcohol on long-haul flights tends to worsen sleep quality and jet lag rather than helping you rest.

## Blood clots

Long periods of sitting slightly increase the risk of a clot in the leg, known as a deep vein thrombosis. For most healthy travellers the risk remains low. It is higher after a previous clot or recent surgery, in pregnancy, with oestrogen-containing medicines, with cancer, or with significant obesity.

- On flights of more than about four hours, walk the aisle when you can and flex your ankles regularly while seated.
- Properly fitted compression stockings reduce the risk for people at higher risk. We will advise whether they make sense for you.
- If you have risk factors, talk to us before a long-haul trip. Occasionally more is needed.

A painful, swollen calf, or sudden breathlessness or chest pain in the days after a flight, needs urgent medical attention.

## Before you go

Check vaccinations early; some need several weeks or more than one dose. Carry medicines in hand luggage in their original packaging, with a copy of the prescription or a doctor's letter, because some countries restrict medicines that are routine in the UK. And know how you would reach medical help at your destination, particularly if you live with a long-term condition.
